# WENDELL ROLLS — Ready-to-Paste ChatGPT Image Prompts

How to use this file: paste the **Style Prefix** once at the start of a new ChatGPT conversation (or resend it if the thread gets long), generate the **Character Sheet** first and get it approved, then work through the **chapter prompts** in order, attaching the character sheet image (and the most recently accepted chapter image) as references each time. Do not skip the character sheet — it's what keeps Wendell, Ruth, Crane, and the rest looking like the same people across 24 images.

This assumes ChatGPT's built-in image tool (GPT Image, formerly branded DALL·E). Full workflow rationale is in `00-style-bible.md`.

---

## Style Prefix — paste verbatim at the top of every single prompt

```
Illustration style: single-color woodblock/linocut print in the American
folk-art tradition — think old almanac woodcuts and WPA-era prints. Bold,
slightly irregular black linework with visible carving texture: rough
edges, occasional gouge marks, ink pooling darker at line intersections.
Palette strictly limited to three colors: bone-white background
(#F5F0E6), lamp-black ink (#1A1A1A), and a single rust-red accent
(#A63A2E) used sparingly, only for emphasis — a lantern's glow, a
sunset band, a stain. No gradients, no soft shading: light and shadow
are hard-edged black shapes, as if lit by one uncompromising light
source with no fill light. Square 1:1 composition, framed inside a
thick black border (about 5% of image width). Figures are slightly
stylized and angular rather than photorealistic, in the manner of folk
printmaking — not cartoonish, not painterly. Mood: plainspoken,
weathered, tender even at its darkest — never horror-movie, never
sentimental.
```

## Character Sheet — generate and lock this before Chapter 1

```
[STYLE PREFIX ABOVE]

Character reference sheet, multi-panel grid on one page, six panels,
each labeled in small plain block capitals at the bottom of its panel:

1. "THE HEAD" — a human head only, no body, roughly the size of a
   cantaloupe, resting upright in tall grass, calm and alert, a plain
   unremarkable boy's face, dark hair, steady eyes, no expression of
   distress.
2. "THE UNFOLDING" — the same head, now with a slender plant-like stalk
   of new form rising and branching from the base of the neck where it
   sealed, arms and a trunk just beginning to unfurl from it like a
   seedling breaking soil, rendered as botanical growth, not surgical
   or anatomical.
3. "WENDELL, BOY" — age seven to twelve, whole body now, thin, in
   oversized hand-me-down clothes that hang loose on him, bare feet or
   ill-fitting boots, plain and unguarded expression.
4. "WENDELL, DRIFTER" — age twelve to seventeen, taller, leaner, road-
   worn, canvas sack over one shoulder, a flat, watchful, unreadable
   expression.
5. "THE FATHER" — a weathered man in feed-mill work clothes, heavyset
   through the shoulders, a face that avoids the viewer's eye, holding
   a metal lunch pail.
6. "THE MOTHER" — a plain rural woman, work-worn hands, sleeves rolled,
   a steady, unsentimental gaze that meets the viewer directly.

Keep all six figures in the same linocut line weight and the same
scale logic, so they can be recombined consistently across later
illustrations.
```

Once this is approved, attach it (plus the most recent chapter image once you have one) to every prompt below.

## Book One — The House

### Chapter 1 — The body unfolding in church light
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a small rural clapboard church, night, empty pews receding
into darkness. A single shaft of colored light falls through one
stained-glass window depicting a shepherd holding a lamb, pooling in
reds and blues on the wooden floor. At the center of that light: a
human head at the base of a slender stalk-like form, arms and a torso
unfolding upward from it like a plant breaking through soil, mid-
transformation, rendered as botanical growth rather than anatomy — no
blood, no surgical detail, just pale new limbs unfurling like shoots.
The shepherd in the window looks down, unmoved, still holding his
glass lamb. Wide shot, the transformation small within the vast dark
church, the one bar of colored light the only source of illumination.
```

### Chapter 2 — First run through the back pasture
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A boy (see reference: Wendell, boy) at a dead run across an open back
pasture at golden hour, captured mid-stride with linocut motion lines
trailing behind his legs and arms. A weathered wood fence recedes
toward a small farmhouse in the distance. A dog is caught mid-leap
beside him, or a scatter of crows lifting off a fence rail ahead of
him. His face is open, unguarded, laughing — the one unclouded moment
of joy in the book. Rust-red accent used only in the low sun on the
horizon line.
```

### Chapter 3 — Leaving at twelve, dirt road at dawn
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A lone boy (Wendell, drifter — just turned twelve, still thin) walking
away down a straight dirt road at first light, seen from behind or
three-quarter behind, a canvas sack slung over one shoulder. His
shadow stretches long behind him across the gravel. A small farmhouse
recedes in the far distance, one window lit. The road vanishes into a
flat, empty horizon. Rust-red used only as a thin band of dawn light
low in the sky.
```

## Book Two — Apprenticeship in Being Seen

### Chapter 4 — The barn, lantern-lit
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a hay barn at night. An older farmer (weathered, work
clothes, not the same man as "the father" in the reference sheet —
give him a thinner build and a flat cap) stands in a hay-loft doorway
holding a lantern in one hand and a shotgun leveled low in the other.
The lantern light falls across a boy (Wendell, boy, dust-covered,
travel-worn) half-risen from a nest of hay, caught between stillness
and flight. Strong single-source lighting: lantern-gold pooling in the
immediate foreground, everything beyond it in hard black shadow.
Tension held in the negative space between the two figures.
```

### Chapter 5 — Munro at the water tower
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Dusk. A wooden rail-line water tower silhouetted against a darkening
sky. Two figures sit near a small, low campfire at its base: an old
drifter (stooped, oversized patchwork coat, weathered face — introduce
this as "Munro" for future consistency) and Wendell (drifter, younger,
listening). Rust-red accent used only in the small fire between them.
Wide shot, the water tower looming large above two small seated
figures, emphasizing scale and isolation.
```

### Chapter 6 — The empty ring
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A dusty small-town square in front of a white clapboard church.
Wendell (drifter) stands alone at the center of a loose, uneven ring
of townspeople — men and women rendered as simple silhouetted shapes,
faces mostly turned or shadowed, all keeping a visible, deliberate
distance from him. The empty space between Wendell and the ring is the
true subject of the composition — wide and stark. No rust-red in this
one; keep it bone-white and black only, flat midday light, no shadows
to hide in.
```

### Chapter 7 — Dry season, night
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Night. A cracked, drought-split cornfield with dead curled stalks.
In the middle distance, a boy (Wendell, drifter) runs low and fast
through the rows, small against the dark. Far behind him, a loose line
of lantern and torch lights strung out across the field, the men
carrying them rendered only as distant black silhouettes. Rust-red
used only in the scattered torchlight, harsh and small against a vast
black field and sky.
```

### Chapter 8 — Vesta Coombs' kitchen
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a small, plain farmhouse kitchen at night, warm lamplight
filling the frame (rust-red accent used generously here, in deliberate
contrast to the previous chapter's darkness). An older widow (plain
dress, hair pulled back, calm competent hands — introduce as "Vesta"
for future consistency) sits across a wooden table from Wendell
(drifter), tending a bandaged hand resting palm-up on the table
between them. Quiet, domestic, unhurried composition. No urgency in
the linework here — softer figure poses than the rest of the book.
```

### Chapter 9 — The medicine show at dusk
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Dusk, a small-town square. A large canvas revival tent glows from
within, a hand-painted banner strung above its entrance (lettering
can be illegible/suggested rather than readable). A dense crowd of
silhouetted townspeople presses toward the tent's open flap. Wendell
(drifter) stands apart at the very edge of the crowd, alone, watching
rather than joining, his posture noticeably still against the crowd's
forward lean. Rust-red used in the tent's interior glow only.
```

## Book Three — The Exhibition

### Chapter 10 — Terms
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of the revival tent, near-empty, after hours. A showman
(sharp-dressed, hat, confident stance — introduce as "Crane" for
future consistency) stands backlit and slightly taller in the
composition, one hand extended toward Wendell (drifter) in a handshake,
an open cashbox visible on a crate between them. Strong single
backlight behind Crane throws him partially into silhouette while
Wendell remains lit and legible — a visual note that Crane holds the
power in this exchange.
```

### Chapter 11 — The living testament
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a packed revival tent at night. Wendell (drifter, now
dressed in a shirt slightly too large for him) stands alone on a
raised wooden platform under hanging lantern light. Below him, a sea
of upturned crowd faces rendered as a dense field of simple
silhouetted heads, all tilted toward him. A banner hangs behind him,
lettering suggested rather than legible. Composition emphasizes his
smallness and aloneness against the scale of the crowd looking up at
him.
```

### Chapter 12 — What it buys
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Close, still-life-style composition on a wooden folding table: a small
stack of coins and a folded bill, beside a hand-lettered testimony
card and a corked bottle of "water." Warm lantern light (rust-red
accent) pools on the table from one side. The objects are arranged
almost tidily — but one element should feel quietly, subtly wrong or
staged (the coins too neatly stacked, the card at a deliberate angle)
to suggest unease beneath the ordinary tableau. No figures in this
one.
```

### Chapter 13 — The ask
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior, dim tent-back space at night. Crane leans in close to
Wendell (drifter), speaking low, his face partly in shadow, one hand
gesturing persuasively. At the very edge of the frame, partially cut
off, a hint of a hidden prop or second figure barely visible in the
dark — enough to suggest concealment without fully explaining it.
Claustrophobic, tight framing compared to earlier wide shots.
```

### Chapter 14 — Walking off the stage
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of the revival tent, mid-performance. Wendell (drifter)
is caught mid-stride walking away from the raised platform toward the
tent's exit, backlit by the stage lanterns behind him so he's a dark
figure moving into shadow. Behind him, the crowd is rendered as a blur
of turned, confused silhouetted heads breaking from their forward-
facing rows. Crane stands rigid near the platform, watching him go.
Sense of motion and reversal — the only image in the book where
Wendell moves toward the viewer rather than away.
```

## Book Four — The Mirror

### Chapter 15 — Ruth Calloway
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A young woman (roughly Wendell's age, plain dress, arms crossed,
leaning in a narrow alley mouth beside a bakery — introduce as "Ruth"
for future consistency) with a wooden prosthetic leg below one knee,
shown plainly and matter-of-factly, not hidden or emphasized as
tragic. Her expression is dry, unimpressed, direct — looking straight
at the viewer (standing in for Wendell). Daytime, ordinary small-town
street background, unremarkable light.
```

### Chapter 16 — Working together
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a modest boardinghouse room, daytime. Ruth and Wendell
(drifter) sit close together at a small table, both bent over mending
work in progress — needles, cloth, a collar half-finished. Ordinary,
companionable, unromantic composition: two people doing a shared task
side by side, matter-of-fact daylight through a window, no dramatic
lighting.
```

### Chapter 17 — The first real fight
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior, a boardinghouse kitchen doorway. Ruth stands on one side of
an open doorframe, Wendell on the other — the doorframe itself is a
visible dividing line in the composition. Both figures' body language
reads as tense, arms folded or turned partly away from each other, a
noticeable gap of empty floor between them despite the small room.
Flat, unforgiving daylight, no warmth in the palette beyond the
minimum required rust-red accent.
```

### Chapter 18 — Staying
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior of a small rented room, evening. Wendell (drifter) sits at a
window, but facing inward into the room rather than looking outward
toward a road — a deliberate reversal of every earlier "road" image in
the book. Soft lamp glow (rust-red accent) lights the room from one
side; the window behind him shows only a dark, undetailed exterior.
Quiet, settled, still composition.
```

### Chapter 19 — The knock
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior, a dim boardinghouse kitchen at night, seen from inside
facing the front door. Two long shadows are cast across the floor from
the doorway — implying figures standing just outside, unseen except
for their cast shapes. Ruth and Wendell stand close together in the
foreground, facing the door, caught in the moment just before it
opens. Minimal light: one low lamp only, heavy shadow dominating the
frame.
```

## Book Five — Reckoning

### Chapter 20 — What followed him
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A small-town street at dusk. In the middle distance, two figures
approach — one sharp-dressed and confident (Crane), one broad and
silent beside him (a hired man, heavyset, plain-faced). In the
foreground, Wendell and Ruth stand together outside a boardinghouse,
already turning to look down the street at the approaching pair.
Long shadows, low sun, a sense of arrival and inevitability.
```

### Chapter 21 — Mercy or the other thing
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Tight, close composition: Wendell's hand, held open and empty in the
air, hovering just short of a fire iron resting against a hearth. His
face is visible just behind the hand, the real subject of the image —
caught mid-decision, not mid-action. Firelight (rust-red accent) is
the only light source, throwing hard shadow across the lower half of
his face. No other figures fully visible in frame; suggestion of a
fallen man's shape at the very edge, out of focus.
```

### Chapter 22 — The cost paid
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Interior, the aftermath of a struggle — an overturned chair, a wrecked
parlor edge visible, but the composition's real focus is a hand
tending a wound: bandaging a shoulder or wrist, close and quiet. Empty
space in the frame where a third presence has just left. Muted, still,
no motion lines — a deliberate contrast to Chapter 21's tension.
```

### Chapter 23 — The house again
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

Exterior, a farmhouse porch and threshold — deliberately matching the
composition of the Chapter 1 illustration's setting. Wendell (drifter,
now visibly older/grown) stands at the same porch steps. Through the
open doorway behind him, the father sits in a chair facing away from
the room, his back to the viewer, unchanged in posture from years
before. A visual rhyme with the book's opening, emphasizing what has
and hasn't changed.
```

### Chapter 24 — Rolling
```
[STYLE PREFIX]

A long, straight dirt road at dawn, matching the composition of the
Chapter 3 illustration — but this time Wendell's shadow falls behind
him rather than stretching out ahead. He walks with steady, forward
motion, sack over one shoulder, expression calm rather than fleeing.
If the ending keeps her: Ruth walks beside him, her prosthetic leg
shown plainly, matching pace. Rust-red used only in a thin dawn-light
band on the horizon, the same as Chapter 3, to complete the visual
bookend.
```

## Notes on iteration

- If a generation drifts off-model (wrong face, wrong palette), don't just regenerate — attach the character sheet again explicitly and say "match the reference character sheet exactly" in your follow-up message.
- Keep the labels ("Wendell, drifter," "Crane," "Ruth") consistent every time you reference them — GPT Image responds better to a stable name/label than to re-describing the same character from scratch each time.
- If GPT Image starts adding color outside the three-color palette, say so directly in an edit turn: "remove all color except bone-white, lamp-black, and rust-red."
